Write a short story in ten scenes

Most good picture-book stories follow the same shape: someone wants something, something gets in the way, and it works out in the end. Ten scenes of one to three sentences each make a story of about one and a half minutes.

A ten-scene plan

  1. Title. The name of the story, over a picture of the main character.
  2. Meet the hero. Who they are and where they are.
  3. What they want. A wish, a worry or a question.
  4. The problem. Something gets in the way.
  5. A first try. It doesn't quite work.
  6. A helper or an idea. A friend, a parent, a teacher or a surprise.
  7. Trying again. This time with help.
  8. The big moment. The problem is solved.
  9. How everyone feels. Happy, proud, thankful.
  10. The lesson or a warm goodbye. One simple sentence to remember.

Words that read aloud well

  • Keep sentences short. One idea per sentence. Aim for 15 to 40 words in each scene.
  • Use names often. "Mira looked outside" is easier for young listeners than "She looked outside".
  • Add sounds and feelings. "Bump!", "Splash!", "Mira sighed." Listeners love them.
  • Repeat a line. A phrase that comes back, like "But the rain would not stop," helps children follow along.
  • End with something to remember. A kind lesson, a Bible verse or a simple "Good night".

Putting it in the app

Tap a scene in the strip, then type in Words & voice. Use + Scene to add a scene, or Duplicate to copy the current one with its picture and characters. You can end the story however you like; nothing is added automatically.
