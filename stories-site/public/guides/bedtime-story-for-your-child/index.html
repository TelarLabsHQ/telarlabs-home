Make a personal bedtime story for your child

Children love hearing stories about themselves. With Telar Stories you can make a short bedtime story where your child is the hero, read in a gentle voice, with soft music and a calm ending.

Pick the ingredients

  • Your child's name, and a friend, sibling or pet who joins the adventure.
  • Something your child loves: rain, stars, animals, the beach, building things, helping in the kitchen.
  • A small worry or a wish: being afraid of the dark, missing a grandparent, starting school, wanting a puppy.
  • A warm ending: feeling safe, being proud, falling asleep.

A simple bedtime plan

  1. Title: "Ella and the Sleepy Stars."
  2. Your child at home in the evening.
  3. Something gentle happens: a star twinkles, the cat wants to play, the rain taps on the window.
  4. A small adventure, two or three scenes. Keep it calm, not scary.
  5. Coming home, a hug from Mom or Dad.
  6. Getting cozy in bed.
  7. "Good night, Ella. Sweet dreams."

Choosing characters

Pick the character who looks most like your child, or the one they like best. Mira and Mei have many poses, and Mei's family includes a mom and a dad. Mochi the cat is a good pet for any story. The name in the words is what makes it your child's story; the picture doesn't have to match exactly.

Making it calm

  • Choose Light: night (darker) on characters in evening scenes, and backgrounds like the cozy bedroom, the reading nook or the starry lake.
  • Use Movement: gentle breathing or steady, not hop.
  • Pick the Slow speed for the AI voice, and the Gentle: Soft piano music.
  • End on a still scene with very few words, so the video fades out softly.

Your own voice

A story read by Mom, Dad, Lola or Lolo means more than any narrator. Record each scene with ● Record my voice. Grandparents who live far away can record a story on their own phone and send the video, a lovely way to keep bedtime together.

Keep it safe

Save the video and also tap Save a backup, so you can change the story later, like adding a new pet or a new year at school. See Sharing your video and keeping your work.

Privacy tip: if you share the video outside the family, consider using a first name only.

Questions parents ask

How long should a bedtime story be?

One to three minutes is plenty. Children often ask to watch it again, which is part of the bedtime routine.

Can I make a story about a hard moment, like a new sibling or moving house?

Yes. Stories help children understand big changes. Keep the problem gentle, show the child feeling unsure, and end with comfort and love.

Is it free?

Yes. Telar Stories is free, with no sign-up, and your stories stay on your device.
