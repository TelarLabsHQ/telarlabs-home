Using story videos in the classroom

A short story video can open a lesson, start a discussion, or become a project students make themselves. Here are ways teachers use Telar Stories in class.

1. A story to start a lesson

Play a one- to two-minute story at the start of a lesson to set the topic. Friends Around the World opens a lesson about cultures and greetings; The Christmas Gift opens a talk about kindness and giving.

2. Listening and comprehension

  • Before: show the title scene and ask, "What do you think this story is about?"
  • During: pause after a key scene. "How does Amara feel now? How do you know?"
  • After: ask students to put three scenes in order, or retell the story in their own words.

3. Values and social-emotional learning

Make a short story about a classroom situation: sharing toys, including a new classmate, saying sorry, being patient. Children can talk about a character's choices more easily than about their own.

4. Language learning

  • Write the same story in two languages, and make one video for each.
  • Use the AI voice's Slow speed so learners can follow every word, with the captions on screen.
  • Students record their own voice reading a scene, a good speaking practice they can listen back to.

5. Students make their own stories

Older children can plan and make a story in groups:

  1. Plan: a hero, a problem, and how it's solved, in six to eight scenes on paper.
  2. Write: one to three sentences per scene. Check spelling together.
  3. Build: choose backgrounds and characters, and place them.
  4. Voice: record the narration, taking turns reading.
  5. Share: play every group's video for the class.

Students don't need accounts. If groups work over several days on a shared computer, use Save a backup at the end of each class and Open a backup to continue.

6. A video for parents

Make a short story about the week's lesson or a class event, and send it to parents. It's an easy way to show what children learned.

Practical tips

  • Download the video before class and play the MP4 file, so it doesn't depend on the school's internet.
  • Keep videos short. Two short stories are better than one long one.
  • For a whiteboard to go with the lesson, try PizarraPad, our free online board.

Questions teachers ask

Do students need accounts or email addresses?

No. Telar Stories works in the browser with no sign-up. Work is saved on the device, and backups can be saved as files.

Does it work on school Chromebooks and tablets?

It works in modern browsers like Chrome, Edge and Safari. The AI voice needs a one-time download of about 90 MB; on slow connections, students can record their own voices instead.

Can I use the videos on our school page?

Yes, the videos you make are yours to share. Follow your school's rules about students' names and voices.
