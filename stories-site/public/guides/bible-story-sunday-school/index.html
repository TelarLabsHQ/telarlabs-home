Make a Bible story video for Sunday school

A short narrated video is a gentle way to open a Sunday school lesson, a children's message or a family devotion. Telar Stories includes three ready-made Bible stories, and you can make your own.

Start with a ready-made story

The First Christmas, The Lost Sheep and Noah's Ark are ready to play. Each has pictures, words and a narrator. Use one as it is, or change the words to match the Bible version your church uses.

Choose a story for your own video

Pick a story with a clear beginning, middle and end, and one main lesson. Stories that work well for young children:

  • Parables: the lost sheep, the lost coin, the good Samaritan, the mustard seed.
  • Old Testament stories: creation, Noah, Joseph's coat, baby Moses, David and Goliath, Daniel and the lions, Jonah.
  • Jesus and children: "Let the little children come to me", feeding the five thousand, calming the storm.

Write it in eight to ten scenes

  1. Title and where the story is found, for example "A story Jesus told. Luke 15."
  2. Setting: who is there and where.
  3. The problem or the question.
  4. What happens next, two or three scenes.
  5. The turning point: God helps, Jesus speaks, someone chooses to obey.
  6. How it ends.
  7. One sentence to remember, like a short verse or "God never stops looking for us."

Use simple words, and keep each scene to one to three sentences. Short sentences are easier for children to follow when they are read aloud.

Reading Scripture aloud

  • Bible names: the AI voice reads most names well. If one sounds wrong, add it to Pronunciation help, for example Goliath = guh-lie-uth. See Voices and pronunciation.
  • Quoting a verse: keep it short, and say where it comes from in the caption.
  • Your own voice: many teachers record the verse themselves and let the AI voice read the story. You can mix both in one video.

Pictures

Use the Bible settings in Story places: the house in Nazareth, the road to Bethlehem, the stable, fields at night, rocky hills, the pasture and the ark scenes. Characters from the ready-made Bible stories, like the shepherds, Noah's family and the animals, can be reused in new stories.

Using it in class

  • Play the video at the start, then ask two or three questions: "Who was lost? Who looked for it? How did the shepherd feel?"
  • Pause before the last scene and ask the children to guess the ending.
  • Send the video to parents afterwards, so families can talk about the story at home.

Tip: Keep videos between one and three minutes. Young children listen best to short stories they can watch more than once.

Questions Sunday school teachers ask

Can I use the ready-made Bible stories in church?

Yes. Play them in class, in children's church or in a service, and share them with families.

Which Bible version are the stories based on?

The ready-made stories retell the Bible in simple words for children. Check the wording against your church's Bible and change any line you like before making your video.

Can children make their own Bible story?

Yes. Older children can write a parable in their own words in six to eight scenes, choose the pictures, and record the narration together.
