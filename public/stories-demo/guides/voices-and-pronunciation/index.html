Voices: the AI storyteller, your own voice, and saying names right

Every scene can have a voice. You can use a free AI storyteller voice, record your own, or mix both in one story.

The AI storyteller voice

In Words & voice, choose a voice such as Warm storyteller (female) or Calm storyteller (male), and a speed: Slow, Normal or Faster. Then tap Make AI voice for this scene, or For all scenes.

  • The AI voice runs inside your browser, so your words aren't sent anywhere.
  • The first time, your device downloads the voice, about 90 MB. Use Wi-Fi if you can. After that it's much faster.
  • Keep the page open until the voices are ready.

Recording your own voice

Tap ● Record my voice, read the scene's words, then tap Stop. Use ▶ Listen to check it. With a recording, the scene lasts as long as your voice plus a short pause.

  • Record in a quiet room, with the phone about a hand's width from your mouth.
  • Read a little slower than you normally speak, and smile; listeners can hear it.
  • Children, parents and grandparents can record their own lines for a family story.

When the AI says a word wrong

Names and words from other languages are often hard for AI voices. Take Mochi, the cat in our stories: the AI voice may say "MOH-chee" or "MOH-kee", but we want "mow-chee". Open Pronunciation help, then write the word, an equals sign, and how it sounds:

Mochi = mowchee

Add more words the same way, one per line:

Mochi = mowchee
lola = law-lah
Lucia = loo-see-ah
  • Use small letters only. Capital letters make the voice spell the word out, like an acronym: "MOW-chee" would come out as "M-O-W chee".
  • Use hyphens to split syllables if it helps.
  • Tap ▶ Hear the words to test, then Save & update voices.
  • Captions on the video keep your original spelling.
