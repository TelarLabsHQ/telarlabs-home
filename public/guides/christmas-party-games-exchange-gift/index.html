Christmas Party Games and Exchange Gift Ideas (Monito-Monita Themes Included)

By the Telar Labs team · November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

A great Christmas party needs three things: a fair gift exchange, a few games everyone can join, and a little suspense at the end. Here’s a ready plan for all three, whether it’s an office party, a class party, a church fellowship or a family reunion.

Step 1: Set up the gift exchange

  1. Agree on a budget. One clear amount keeps it fair, for example $10 or $20 (₱300 or ₱500 is common in the Philippines).
  2. Draw names. Use the free Secret Santa draw: type the names, add pairs who shouldn’t pick each other (couples, siblings), and send each person a private link that shows only their pick. No sign-up, and the organizer can join too.
  3. Ask for wish lists. Everyone posts three gift ideas in the group chat, without saying who they drew.
  4. Set the exchange date and plan one or two spare gifts for anyone whose giver can’t come.

Monito-Monita: a theme for each week

In the Philippines, many groups exchange small gifts every week before the party, each with a theme. Pick four or five, one per week, and keep each one small:

  • Something soft – socks, a small pillow, a scarf, a plush toy
  • Something sweet – chocolates, a jar of cookies, local delicacies
  • Something red or something green
  • Something that starts with the first letter of your name (or your Monito’s name)
  • Something you can read – a book, a magazine, a planner
  • Something hot – coffee, hot sauce, instant noodles, a mug
  • Something useful at work or school – pens, sticky notes, a desk organizer
  • Something homemade – baked goods, a card, a small craft
  • Something that makes you smile – a funny mug, a joke book
  • Something long or something round – for the creative ones

Save the main gift, within the full budget, for the party itself.

Exchange gift ideas by budget

Around $5 (₱150–₱250)

  • Gourmet chocolate bar or a bag of local snacks
  • Cute socks or a pocket notebook
  • Lip balm, hand cream or a small hand sanitizer set
  • A succulent in a small pot

Around $10 (₱300–₱500)

  • Mug with coffee, tea or hot chocolate
  • Tumbler or reusable water bottle
  • Phone stand, cable organizer or a pop socket
  • Puzzle, card game or a pack of good pens

Around $20 (₱800–₱1,000)

  • Bluetooth earbuds or a small speaker (look for good-value brands)
  • Scented candle set or a cozy blanket
  • Desk lamp, planner or a nice wallet
  • A book from their wish list, with a handwritten note

Tip: when in doubt, choose something useful, consumable or easy to re-gift, and include a gift receipt if you can.

Party games everyone can join

For any group

  • Bring Me. The host calls out items (“a red pen,” “a photo of your pet,” “a receipt from this week”), and the first person or team to bring it wins a point. Fast, loud and great for icebreaking.
  • Paper Dance. Pairs dance on a sheet of newspaper. Each time the music stops, they fold it in half. Last pair standing wins.
  • Christmas Charades. Act out carols, Christmas movies or holiday words without speaking.
  • Gift Wrap Race. Teams wrap a box as fast as they can, with one hand each, or while blindfolded.
  • Name That Carol. Hum or play the first few notes of a Christmas song; the first to guess the title wins.
  • Christmas Trivia. 10 to 15 questions about holiday traditions around the world. Play in teams.

For offices and adults

  • White Elephant swap. Everyone brings a wrapped gift. Players take turns opening a new gift or “stealing” one already opened. Each gift can be stolen up to three times.
  • Guess Who Wrote It. Everyone writes an unusual fact about themselves on a card; read them out and guess who wrote each one.
  • Best Holiday Outfit. A simple contest with a small prize, voted by applause.

For kids and classes

  • Musical Chairs with Christmas songs.
  • Pass the Parcel. A small prize wrapped in many layers; one layer comes off each time the music stops.
  • Christmas Bingo with holiday pictures instead of numbers.
  • Pin the Nose on the Reindeer. A blindfold classic.

Running the games: free tools that help

  • Brackets for team games: the bracket maker sets up a knockout or round robin in seconds. Tap the winners as you go, and show it on a TV.
  • Door prizes and raffles: print numbered tickets with the raffle ticket maker, then pick winners on the big screen with the raffle draw. It can also draw from a list of names, so every guest gets a chance without tickets.
  • A countdown or “starting soon” screen: free moving backgrounds and countdowns from Telar Clips make the room feel ready while people arrive.

A simple party timeline

  1. 4 weeks before: set the date, place and budget, and draw names.
  2. 3 to 1 weeks before: weekly Monito-Monita themes; collect contributions if needed.
  3. Party day, first hour: welcome, food, and one icebreaker game.
  4. Second hour: two or three games, then the gift exchange.
  5. Last 15 minutes: raffle and door prizes, group photo, thank-yous.
