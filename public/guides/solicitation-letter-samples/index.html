Solicitation Letter Samples for Churches, Schools and Communities (Free to Copy)

By the Telar Labs team · November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

A good solicitation letter is short, specific and honest: who you are, what the money is for, how much you need, and exactly how to give. Below are four samples you can copy and change, a simple structure for writing your own, and tips for sending it so people respond.

The 6 parts of a solicitation letter

  1. Letterhead and date. Your organization’s name, address or contact number, and the date.
  2. Greeting. Use the person’s name when you can (“Dear Mr. and Mrs. Lee,”). People respond far more to a letter addressed to them than to “Dear Sir/Madam.”
  3. Who you are and the need. One or two sentences on your group, then the problem in plain words.
  4. The ask, with a number. How much you need in total, what it pays for, and a few example amounts (“$10 buys a box of school supplies”).
  5. How to give and the deadline. Every way to give, and the date you need it by.
  6. Thanks and signature. A warm thank-you, then the name and role of the person signing.

Keep it to one page. If people need more detail, they can ask, or you can link to a page with photos and updates.

Sample 1: Church building project

HILLSIDE CHAPEL BUILDING COMMITTEE
12 Hill Road, Bayview · 0775 218 4406

[Date]

Dear Brothers and Sisters in Christ,

Greetings of peace!

For more than 60 years, Hillside Chapel has been a place of worship, baptisms and fellowship for our community. Today, our roof leaks every rainy season, right above the children’s choir corner, and the damage is getting worse.

We need $2,000 to replace the roofing sheets and gutters before the next storm season. We humbly ask for your generous support. Every amount helps: $10 buys a roofing screw set, $50 covers a full roofing sheet, and $100 pays for a day of labor.

You can give online at [your donation link], by bank transfer to [account details], or in person after Sunday Mass. We hope to complete the project by [date], and we will share receipts and photos of the work with everyone who gives.

Thank you, and may God bless you and your family.

Yours in Christ,

[Name]
Building Committee Chair

Sample 2: School or PTA fundraiser

RIVERSIDE ELEMENTARY SCHOOL PARENTS–TEACHERS ASSOCIATION
45 School Street, Riverside · riverside.pta@email.com

[Date]

Dear Parents, Alumni and Friends of Riverside Elementary,

Our Grade 5 and 6 classrooms share just 12 computers among 180 students. Many children only get to use one for 20 minutes a week, while more and more of their lessons and projects need them.

This year, the PTA aims to raise $3,000 for 10 new laptops and a charging cart for the library. Any gift makes a difference: $5 helps buy a pair of headphones, $25 buys a mouse and keyboard set, and $100 covers a quarter of a laptop.

Please give by [deadline] at [your donation link], or send your donation in a sealed envelope with your child’s name and section to the class adviser. Every gift will be listed (you may choose to stay anonymous), and we will post the full report at the end of the drive.

Thank you for investing in our children’s learning.

Sincerely,

[Name]
PTA President

Sample 3: Community relief drive

BAYVIEW NEIGHBORHOOD ASSOCIATION
Community Hall, 3 Harbor Lane · 0775 402 1187

[Date]

Dear Neighbors and Friends,

Last week’s flood forced 46 families in the Lower Bayview area to leave their homes. Many lost food, clothes, school supplies and bedding.

We are preparing relief packs for every affected family: rice, canned goods, drinking water, hygiene kits and school supplies. Each pack costs about $25. Our goal is 50 packs, or $1,250, by [date].

You can give any amount at [your donation link], or bring cash or goods to the Community Hall from 8 AM to 5 PM. All donations are recorded, and we will post the list of packs delivered, with photos, once the drive is complete.

Thank you for standing with our neighbors when they need it most.

With gratitude,

[Name]
Relief Drive Coordinator

Sample 4: Medical help for a family

[Date]

Dear Friends and Family,

Many of you know Daniel Reyes, our coworker, coach and friend for over ten years. Last month, Daniel was diagnosed with kidney failure and now needs dialysis three times a week while he waits for a transplant.

The treatment costs about $600 a month, and Daniel cannot work full-time during this period. We, his friends from [group or office], are raising $3,600 to cover six months of treatment.

If you can, please give at [your donation link]. Daniel’s family has agreed to this appeal, and every gift goes directly to his wife, Grace. We will share updates on Daniel’s recovery and a full record of the gifts received.

Thank you for your prayers and kindness.

Sincerely,

[Name]
On behalf of Daniel’s friends at [group or office]

Tips that make people give

  • Be specific. “$2,000 for new roofing sheets” works better than “help us repair the chapel.”
  • Show what each amount does. Small examples like “$10 buys…” make giving feel concrete, and they help people who can only give a little.
  • Set a deadline. A date gives people a reason to act now instead of “later.”
  • Promise a report, and keep the promise. Say you’ll share receipts or a list of gifts. People give again to groups that report back.
  • Get permission. For a person or family in need, make sure they agree to the appeal and to what you share about them.
  • Follow up once. A short reminder a week before the deadline brings in many late gifts.

Paper, email or online?

Printed letters still matter in many churches and communities, especially for older members and formal requests. But mailing letters and waiting for envelopes is slow, and cash in envelopes is hard to track.
