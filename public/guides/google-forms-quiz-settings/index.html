Google Forms Quiz Settings That Reduce Cheating

By the Telar Labs team · November 20, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Google Forms is free, familiar and quick to grade, which is why so many teachers use it for quizzes. A few settings, most of them turned off by default, make a big difference to how easy it is to share answers.

This checklist goes through the settings in the order you'll find them. Menu names can change a little as Google updates Forms, but the options have stayed largely the same for years.

Step 1: Turn the form into a quiz

Open your form, go to the Settings tab and switch on Make this a quiz. This lets you set an answer key and point values, and it unlocks the options for releasing grades.

Step 2: Release grades later

Under Release grades, choose Later, after manual review instead of Immediately after each submission.

Why it matters: if scores and correct answers appear the moment a student submits, the first student to finish can share every answer with the rest of the class. Releasing grades after everyone has finished closes that gap. Below this setting, you can also choose what students see when grades are released: missed questions, correct answers and point values. For a quiz you might reuse, consider not showing the correct answers at all.

Step 3: Collect names properly

Under Responses, decide how you'll know who answered. If your students have school Google accounts, you can collect email addresses automatically. If not, add a required short-answer question for the full name and another for the section or class, at the very top of the form.

Step 4: Limit to one response

Turn on Limit to 1 response. This stops a student from submitting once to see the answers and then submitting again. Note that this setting requires students to sign in with a Google account. If your students don't have accounts, you'll need to check for duplicate names yourself in the responses sheet.

Step 5: Shuffle the question order

In the Presentation section of Settings, turn on Shuffle question order. Two students sitting side by side will now see questions in a different order, which makes glancing at a neighbor's screen much less useful.

One catch: shuffling works within a section. If your quiz has several sections, each section's questions are shuffled, but the sections themselves stay in order. For most quizzes, one section is simpler.

Step 6: Shuffle the answer options

For each multiple-choice or checkbox question, open the three-dot menu at the bottom right of the question and choose Shuffle option order. Now "number 3 is B" means nothing, because each student's B is different.

Avoid options like "All of the above" or "Both A and B" when you shuffle, since the letters will move. Write "All of these" or name the options instead.

Step 7: Make answers harder to search

Settings only do so much. If a question can be pasted into a search engine and answered in five seconds, it will be. A few ways to fix that:

  • Add a short scenario before the question, using details from your own lesson.
  • Use an image you made, like a photo of a diagram you drew on the board.
  • Follow important multiple-choice questions with a short "Why?" paragraph answer.
  • Use the Short answer type with response validation for numbers, so students must work out the value rather than pick it.

Step 8: Use locked mode if your school has managed Chromebooks

If your school uses Google Workspace for Education and students take quizzes on school-managed Chromebooks, Forms offers locked mode. It stops students from opening other tabs or apps until they submit. It only works on managed Chromebooks, so it won't help when students use phones, laptops or home computers.

What Google Forms can't do on its own

  • No live view of who left the quiz. Outside locked mode, Forms can't tell you when a student switched to another tab or app.
  • No time limit. Forms has no built-in timer. You can open and close the form by hand with Accepting responses, but there's no countdown for each student.
  • No random question pools. Every student gets the same questions, just in a different order.

Our free tool QuizPolice fills the first two gaps for any device. You paste your Google Forms link, students join with a code and their name, and the quiz opens inside QuizPolice. You see live who leaves the quiz tab and for how long, you can lock students after a set number of exits, and you can add a time limit or schedule the exam so everyone starts together. Before you share the link

  1. Take the quiz yourself in a private browser window, as a student would.
  2. Check that names are required and shown at the start.
  3. Check that the score does not appear when you submit.
  4. Delete your test response from the Responses tab.
  5. Close the form after the quiz with Accepting responses, so late answers can't come in after answers are shared.

Settings won't stop every determined student, but together they remove the easy shortcuts. For the bigger picture, including question writing and how to talk to students, see How to reduce cheating in online quizzes.
